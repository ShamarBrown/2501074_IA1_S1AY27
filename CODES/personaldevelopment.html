5 laws of gold

Book of Reflection: The Richest Man in Babylon

by George S Clason

7 Cures for a Lean Purse

According to the notes from the chapter entitled “Seven Cures for a Lean Purse,” a wealthy person is someone who is well-disciplined, constantly self-educated, and well-planned. In my life, it would start with the mandate to “start thy purse to fattening” that requires me to put at least a 10 th part of all the earnings aside before embarking on the allocation of money toward day-to-day needs and desires. Second, I would always endeavor to improve my ability to earn by furthering technical expertise and acquiring knowledge and good habits as a manner of constant self-education as advised by Arkad. The most valuable advice I have got from the chapter is that wealth requires following basic common-sense principles of living within one’s means, letting one’s earnings earn, protecting one’s earnings from oneself, and educating oneself.













5 Laws of Gold

The story about Nomasir and his experiences with money illustrates how easily one can get deceived by some investment opportunities and, eventually, loose all their hard-earned money. When talking about the five laws of gold, one should remember that in their real life, they should not invest their effort and money in some easy schemes promised by various swindlers. Instead, they should rely solely on those who have relevant experience in the field and, thus, have a greater understanding of what to do and how to act in specific situations. The example of Nomasir illustrates well how the wrong decision made at the wrong time could spoil all the best plans made carefully and in detail. The main message of this story is that wisdom is more valuable than gold coins and, if one has it, they can build their wealth no matter what their starting point is. On the contrary, if they have a lot of money but no relevant experience and no good and trustworthy advisers, all their wealth may be gone in one unfortunate adventure.
